# YOSHRUN — Story & Worldbuilding
**Version:** 0.1 — Original setting

## Premise
The player wakes beneath a pale artificial dawn in a place known only as **The Vast**: a colossal stone labyrinth surrounded by a wilderness that seems to move when nobody is watching. No one remembers arriving. Old mechanisms still operate, gates open on impossible schedules, and distant calls echo through sealed corridors.

The maze is not merely a prison. It is a machine built to observe how people respond to fear, scarcity and cooperation. Who built it, what lies beyond it, and why the player was selected form the central mystery.

## Originality note
YOSHRUN is an original YOSHA property. Its characters, locations, creature designs, symbols, dialogue and plot must be developed independently. The project may draw on broad survival, mystery and labyrinth genre themes, but must not reproduce protected characters, scenes, story beats, names, logos or visual designs from existing films or games.

## World zones
- **The Outer Ring:** a comparatively navigable perimeter used to teach movement, landmarks and basic survival.
- **The Thorn Courts:** overgrown courtyards where vegetation hides routes and sound carries unpredictably.
- **The Sunken Works:** maintenance tunnels, water channels and ancient machinery.
- **The Silent Spires:** vertical stone structures containing observation devices and sealed records.
- **The Heart Gate:** the central mechanism connected to the maze's purpose.

## Narrative chapters
### Chapter I — THE MAZE
The player learns the rules of the labyrinth, meets other survivors, discovers that route changes follow a pattern, and finds evidence of an unseen organization. The chapter ends with a difficult escape beyond the outer wall.

### Chapter II — WORLD COLLAPSE
The wilderness outside is not freedom. The survivors find abandoned infrastructure, scattered communities and signs that the maze is one part of a much larger system. The player must decide whom to trust while tracing the signal that controls the gates.

### Chapter III — FINAL DESTINY
The player reaches the source of the system, confronts its creators or successors, and learns the truth behind the experiments. The finale resolves the central conflict and gives the player's choices a clear consequence.

## Narrative delivery
- Environmental storytelling through markings, damaged devices, architecture and audio logs.
- Short, optional dialogue scenes to preserve player agency.
- Story objectives integrated into exploration rather than long exposition.
- Character motivations and faction identities to be defined before voice recording.

## Creature design principles
Every creature must have:
- A unique silhouette and movement language.
- A recognizable sound cue.
- A clear hunting pattern and readable attack telegraph.
- A weakness or escape countermeasure.
- A distinct ecological or narrative reason to exist.

Creature names and final designs remain to be created. Do not lock placeholder concepts as canon until visual and gameplay prototypes are reviewed.

## Open narrative questions
1. Who constructed the maze and who maintains it now?
2. What makes the maze change its routes?
3. Why was the player selected?
4. What is the relationship between the maze and the outside world's collapse?
5. Which player decisions meaningfully affect the finale?
